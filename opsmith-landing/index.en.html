    How a build runs

    Three weeks, in this order.

    The sequence matters: we don't write a line of agent code until we've done the work by hand and know what "right" looks like.

    01 / WEEK ONE

    Sit in the queue

    Two of our engineers work your queue alongside your team. We write the runbook from what actually happens — including the exceptions nobody documented — and we agree on one pass/fail number before anything is built.

    02 / WEEK TWO

    Shadow on live volume

    The agent runs on your real queue with every side effect disabled. Its decision sits next to your team's on each item. You see the disagreement rate daily, with the reasoning on the ones it got wrong.

    03 / WEEK THREE

    Ship the slice it wins

    We turn it on for the portion of volume where it beat the baseline, with the escalation thresholds you set. The rest keeps going to your team until the next slice clears the bar.

    The parts people ask about

    Built to be stopped.

    Thresholds you set, in your words

    Dollar limits, confidence floors, named accounts, anything touching a person's money or status. Below the line it runs; above it, a named human gets it with the full context attached.

    An audit log that replays

    Every input, decision, tool call and handoff is recorded and re-runnable. When someone asks why an invoice got coded that way in March, you answer in a minute, not a week.

    Your systems, your data

    Scoped credentials per workflow, least privilege, revocable in one click. Your data is never used to train a model. Deploy in our cloud or inside your VPC. SOC 2 Type II audit underway.

    A kill switch that means it

    One switch per workflow, and the queue falls straight back to your team's existing process — the same inbox, the same tooling. No migration to unwind, no lock-in to argue about.

    Questions we get

    Straight answers.

    What counts as one workflow?
    One trigger, one outcome, one owner. "Invoices arriving in ap@ get matched, coded and posted" is a workflow. "Finance" is not. If your process forks into two genuinely different outcomes, that's usually two workflows — we'll tell you in week one, before you pay for the second.
    Is this a layoff tool?
    Not how we sell it, and not how it plays out. In every pilot so far the same team handled more volume and spent their time on the 3% of cases that actually needed judgement. If you're buying this to cut a team in half, we're probably the wrong vendor — the work still needs someone accountable for it.
    What happens when the agent gets something wrong?
    You see it in the ledger with the full reasoning and the inputs that led there. Anything reversible is reversed by the same agent; anything not is flagged to the named owner within the run. We review every escalation weekly for the first quarter and tighten the thresholds with you. Errors above your agreed rate mean the workflow goes back to shadow mode — at our cost.
    Which systems do you connect to?
    The usual back office: NetSuite, QuickBooks, SAP Business One, Zendesk, Intercom, Salesforce, HubSpot, Shopify, ShipStation, Snowflake, Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, plus anything with a REST API or an SFTP drop. If it only has a UI, we drive the UI — slower, still works. Week one tells us which.
    Why the waitlist instead of just selling it?
    Because two engineers sit in your queue for a week, and we only have so many of them. Twenty teams per cohort is the honest number. When that stops being the constraint, the waitlist goes away.
